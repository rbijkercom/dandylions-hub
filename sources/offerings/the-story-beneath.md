## Headline

Every team under pressure tells itself a story. The one that shapes the work is rarely told out loud.

Under high stakes, uncertainty or restructuring, tension is turned into stories: about who is to blame, who carries the load and how it will end. Left unexamined, these stories surface as passive resistance, scapegoating, cynicism or groupthink. The Story Beneath brings them to light and offers teams a safe space to examine them, adjust how they work together, and restore authority and alignment.

## Who it's for

- Executive teams carrying high stakes, where the confidence in the boardroom no longer matches what is said in the corridor
- Project teams under pressure, where project managers, PMO and technical leads have started to see each other as the problem
- Teams in the middle of a restructuring, merger or change of direction
- Sponsors and leaders who sense a gap between the official story and what people actually believe

## What we can work on

- The gap between the official story and the story beneath it
- Who is cast as hero, villain, martyr or invisible worker, and what that casting protects the team from
- The anxieties driving the team's current narrative
- Blurred role boundaries between executives, project managers and support roles
- Reconnecting the team to its primary task
- Agreements on how to communicate when pressure rises again

## How it works

1. **Intake**: We map the team's context, the stakes and the question it is facing.
2. **Narrative audit**: Confidential storytelling interviews across the hierarchy reveal the stories that shape the work.
3. **The narrative mirror**: In a workshop, we reflect those stories back to the team in anonymised form and hold the space to look at them together.
4. **The next chapter**: We translate what surfaced into clearer roles, a sharper shared task and agreements for the next phase.

## Common questions

**Will what people tell you stay confidential?**
Yes. Everything stays within the team. However, the team works with real fantasies, patterns, characters and themes, not abstract fiction.

**Is this a form of therapy?**
No. The work draws on systems psychodynamics and narrative approaches, but its focus is the team, its roles and its task.

**Does it work for both executive and project teams?**
Yes. The stories differ, and so does the focus, but the method is the same: surface the narrative, examine it together, and rewrite the next chapter.
