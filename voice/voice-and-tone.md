# DandyLions voice and tone

Grounded in the published site copy (https://dandy-lions.vercel.app, EN/IT/NL). Every example below is quoted from the site. Full texts are in `sources/`.

## Who is speaking

- **Fabio Bortolazzi**, founder, speaks as **"I"**: the founder statement, Project Coaching ("I don't take the work off your desk or hand you my plan"), the contact page ("I read every message and reply as a person, not a ticket queue"), Policy Compass ("The point isn't that I stay").
- **DandyLions**, the practice, speaks as **"we"**: the services, the method and the consulting home ("We help people and organisations navigate complexity, withstand tension and explore new horizons, with clarity and momentum.").
- The reader is **"you"**: a project manager, director, executive team, policy maker or team under pressure.

For social posts: personal reflections, field observations and event invitations go in Fabio's "I". Descriptions of services and the method go in DandyLions' "we". Don't mix the two in one sentence.

## Tone

Calm, precise, warm and a little literary. It names tension without drama and treats the reader as a capable professional.

- **Name the real situation, concretely.** "A stakeholder who keeps changing their mind, a scope that has quietly doubled, and a delivery date nobody believes in anymore." "We start with whatever landed in your inbox this morning."
- **Define by contrast.** "It isn't training." / "It isn't consulting." "A boundary is not a wall. It is the place where you can still be reached and still be yourself." "A mission is not an assignment."
- **Reframe the problem.** "The issue is not that organizations do not have solutions; it's that they often do not know what causes their problems." "The fix isn't a better template."
- **Short lines with weight.** "If you recognised yourself in this, the work has begun." "Legacy isn't a mausoleum, but a root system."
- **Botanical and craft imagery**, used sparingly: roots, seeds, sowing, blossoming (dandelion); weaving and threads (Pattern Weaver, "The weaver mind"); the recipe, tasting and adjusting (Collaborative Kitchen).
- **A light, human wink** now and then: "No one likes getting older, but growing is a blast."
- **Invitation, not pressure.** The call to action is "Begin the conversation".

## Vocabulary

Use: clarity, direction, momentum; tension; complexity; technical vs adaptive problems; system, roles, boundaries, the primary task; stakeholders; sponsor; loyal dissent; "hold" tension or complexity; reflection; "in action".

Named ideas that belong to DandyLions or that the site cites:

- **Ready · Set · Go → Clarity · Direction · Momentum** (the way of working).
- **Technical vs adaptive problems**: sense–analyze–respond vs probe–sense–respond. Quote on the site from Ronald Heifetz.
- **Four competing loyalties**: the executive top, the end users, the direct manager, the law and governance.
- **Policy Compass**: an instrument developed by the Dutch government. Also mentioned: Opgavegericht werken.
- **Keynote · Masterclass · Training · Learning-on-the-job** = the spark, the map, the practice, long-lasting impact.
- The work draws on **systems psychodynamics and narrative approaches** (The Story Beneath).

## Style rules

- British spelling: organisation, programme, recognise, behaviour. (The site has a few "-ize" spellings; prefer "-ise".)
- No exclamation marks. The site copy has none.
- No emojis on the site. On social, use none, or at most one, and only if Ruben's hashtag/emoji policy allows it. [TO BE SUPPLIED BY RUBEN: emoji and hashtag policy.]
- Em dashes are used sparingly on the site; prefer commas, full stops or a colon in posts.
- Questions are welcome as openers or section heads ("What happens here?", "What do we do about it?").
- Bold only for key nouns, as in "for **boards** and **executive teams**".
- Avoid hype words and growth-hacking phrasing: no "game-changer", "unlock", "10x", "synergy", "hack", "crush it". Avoid generic coaching clichés that the site never uses.
